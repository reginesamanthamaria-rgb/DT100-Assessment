THE HISTORY OF ITF TAEKWON-DO

Discover the origins, development, and philosophy of International Taekwon-Do Federation (ITF) and learn how this martial art has grown from its beginnings in South Korea into a worldwide discipline built upon technique, discipline, and personal development.

Origins of ITF Taekwon-Do

The International Taekwon-Do Federation (ITF) was officially founded on 22 March 1966 in Seoul, South Korea, by General Choi Hong Hi, who is widely recognised as the founder of Taekwon-Do. Before creating the ITF, General Choi spent many years studying martial arts and developing a system that combined traditional Korean martial arts with scientific principles of movement. His goal was to create a martial art that was effective for self-defence while also encouraging discipline, respect, and personal growth.

The development of Taekwon-Do was influenced by General Choi's belief that martial arts should be used not only to strengthen the body, but also to develop the mind and character. Unlike a practice focused only on physical ability, Taekwon-Do was designed to teach students important values that could be applied in everyday life. Through consistent training, practitioners learn responsibility, perseverance, confidence, and respect for others.

The name "Taekwon-Do" reflects the three foundations of the martial art. "Tae" refers to foot techniques and movement, "Kwon" represents hand techniques and striking, and "Do" refers to the journey of personal development and self-improvement. Together, these elements represent the belief that Taekwon-Do is both a physical discipline and a way of developing a person's character.

When the International Taekwon-Do Federation was established, one of its main purposes was to preserve and organise the techniques, patterns (Tul), and philosophy of Taekwon-Do. The organisation began with representatives from nine countries: South Korea, Vietnam, Malaysia, Singapore, West Germany, the United States, Turkey, Italy, and Egypt. From these beginnings, ITF Taekwon-Do began expanding beyond Korea and becoming an international martial art.

International Expansion

Following its establishment, the International Taekwon-Do Federation continued to grow through demonstrations, seminars, instructor courses, and international competitions. General Choi and other senior instructors travelled to different countries to introduce Taekwon-Do and train instructors who could continue teaching the martial art within their own communities.

Over the following decades, ITF Taekwon-Do expanded throughout Asia, Europe, Oceania, Africa, and the Americas. As more clubs and organisations were created, students from different cultures and backgrounds were able to learn the same techniques, patterns, and principles while becoming part of a worldwide martial arts community.

International events became an important part of maintaining connections between practitioners. Seminars allowed students and instructors to develop their understanding of techniques and theory, while competitions provided opportunities to test skills and celebrate achievement. These events helped maintain the global identity of ITF Taekwon-Do.

Training Philosophy

ITF Taekwon-Do focuses on developing both physical ability and personal character. Training includes fundamental movements, patterns (Tul), step sparring, free sparring, self-defence, breaking techniques, fitness, and theory. Each area of training contributes to improving coordination, strength, confidence, and understanding of the martial art.

A key principle of ITF Taekwon-Do is that training should develop the whole individual. While students improve their technical skills, they are also encouraged to develop discipline, patience, responsibility, and respect. These values extend beyond the dojang and can influence how practitioners approach challenges in everyday life.

Students progress through a structured belt system where each grading represents growth in both skill and personal development. Progression requires dedication, effort, and perseverance, reinforcing the idea that improvement comes from consistent practice and commitment.

ITF Taekwon-Do Today

Today, ITF Taekwon-Do continues to be practised by students across the world. Although the martial art has developed significantly since its creation, its original focus on technique, discipline, and personal improvement remains central to its identity.

Modern ITF clubs continue to provide opportunities for students of all ages and experience levels to learn martial arts in a supportive environment. Through regular training, seminars, gradings, and competitions, practitioners continue the traditions established by General Choi while developing their own skills and confidence.

The continued growth of ITF Taekwon-Do demonstrates the lasting influence of a martial art built not only on physical techniques, but also on values that encourage respect, perseverance, and lifelong learning.
