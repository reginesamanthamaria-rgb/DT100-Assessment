The History of ITF

The International Taekwon-Do Federation (ITF) is one of the world's largest and most influential Taekwon-Do organisations. It was founded on 22 March 1966 in Seoul, South Korea, by General Choi Hong Hi, who is widely recognised as the founder of Taekwon-Do. After years of studying martial arts and developing new techniques based on scientific principles of movement, General Choi sought to create a distinctly Korean martial art that emphasised not only physical skill but also discipline, respect, and moral character.

The ITF was established with representatives from nine countries—South Korea, Vietnam, Malaysia, Singapore, West Germany, the United States, Turkey, Italy, and Egypt. Its purpose was to standardise the teaching of Taekwon-Do, preserve its original techniques and philosophy, and promote the martial art internationally. Through demonstrations, instructor training courses, seminars, and competitions, the ITF played a significant role in introducing Taekwon-Do to countries around the world.

As the organisation grew, the ITF expanded rapidly beyond Korea, establishing affiliated organisations across Asia, Europe, Oceania, Africa, and the Americas. Millions of practitioners now train in ITF Taekwon-Do, making it one of the most widely practised Korean martial arts. Although the organisation has experienced changes and divisions throughout its history, its commitment to preserving General Choi's original technical system and philosophy has remained central to its identity.

Unlike many martial arts that focus primarily on competition, ITF Taekwon-Do emphasises the development of both physical ability and personal character. Training includes fundamental movements, patterns (Tul), sparring, self-defence, breaking techniques, and theory, all designed to improve coordination, strength, discipline, and confidence. Students progress through a structured belt system while learning the importance of continuous improvement and lifelong learning.

At the heart of ITF Taekwon-Do are the Five Tenets—Courtesy, Integrity, Perseverance, Self-Control, and Indomitable Spirit. These principles guide practitioners both inside and outside the dojang, encouraging respect for others, honesty, determination, and resilience. Today, the ITF continues to promote General Choi Hong Hi's vision by providing opportunities for students of all ages to develop not only as martial artists but also as responsible and respectful members of their communities.

About The Club

Club Information

Our Taekwon-Do club is a student-focused school community dedicated to helping students develop physical strength, practical self-defence skills, confidence, and resilience in a welcoming and supportive environment. Whether you are stepping onto the mats for the very first time, looking for an enjoyable way to stay active with friends, or hoping to progress through the belt ranks towards black belt, our club provides a place where every student can learn, challenge themselves, and grow at their own pace.

As an affiliated International Taekwon-Do Federation (ITF) club, our training follows internationally recognised standards and the teachings established by General Choi Hong Hi. Every class is carefully structured to develop strong technical foundations while encouraging continuous improvement. Students practise a wide range of skills, including fundamental hand and foot techniques, patterns (Tul), step sparring, free sparring, self-defence, flexibility, fitness, and theory. By combining these areas of training, students build coordination, balance, strength, discipline, and confidence that extend well beyond the dojang.

Taekwon-Do is more than simply learning how to kick and punch. It is a martial art built upon values that encourage personal growth and lifelong learning. At the heart of every lesson are the Five Tenets of Taekwon-Do—Courtesy, Integrity, Perseverance, Self-Control, and Indomitable Spirit. These principles guide how we train, support one another, and approach challenges both inside and outside the dojang. Through consistent practice, students learn the importance of respect, honesty, determination, emotional control, and resilience—qualities that are just as valuable in everyday life as they are in martial arts.

Throughout the year, members have opportunities to participate in coloured belt gradings, seminars, demonstrations, and tournaments, allowing them to apply their skills, gain valuable experience, and celebrate their progress. Every achievement, whether it is earning a new belt, mastering a pattern, or simply building confidence to try something new, is recognised as an important milestone in each student's martial arts journey. Above all, our club strives to create an inclusive, respectful, and encouraging community where every WEGC student feels welcome, regardless of age, experience, or ability. We believe that everyone has the potential to succeed with dedication and perseverance, and we are committed to helping each member become the best version of themselves. Together, we support one another, celebrate each other's achievements, and foster friendships that make every training session both rewarding and enjoyable.
